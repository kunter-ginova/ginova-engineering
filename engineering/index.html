Engineering / GiNOVA

Process and system design for production decisions.

Scoped technical studies for process analysis, capacity assessment and equipment decisions.

Start a technical discussion

Who this is for

Food producers, plant owners, production managers and investors evaluating a production facility or line.

The decision to resolve

Capacity uncertainty, equipment-selection risk, process inefficiency or an incomplete production design.

Outputs to agree

  • Process map with assumptions and interfaces
  • Capacity scenarios and an equipment-requirement matrix
  • Technical specifications and a feasibility assessment
  • A product, prototype or trial plan

Each engagement starts with a written scope: inputs, deliverables, responsibilities, timing, price and acceptance criteria. Examples describe scope, not promised results.

Production-facility studies concern processes, lines, equipment and technical coordination. They do not imply building construction, turnkey contracting or in-house authority for every regulated discipline.

Work requiring regulated professional preparation, signature or approval is subject to the relevant authorized professionals and permissions.

Select a process, then explore the technical points, work and deliverables.

01

Sourcing and supply-flow coordination

Best suited forAn international ingredient or intermediate-product requirement with several producer or processing options.

Critical technical points

Grade, composition, quality tolerances and processing behaviour must fit the receiving operation. Seasonality, batch traceability, intermediate processing and logistics can change the supply decision.

Engineering work

Translate the purchasing need into a technical enquiry; compare producer responses against shared criteria; coordinate samples and record responsibilities at each handover.

Decision deliverables

A specification and producer comparison, sample-approval record and supply responsibility map.

02

Salad and fresh-cut processing

Best suited forA new or upgraded salad and fresh-cut line where quality and handling constraints must guide equipment selection.

Critical technical points

Cut geometry and tissue damage affect oxidation and product quality. Wash-water management, dewatering, cold-chain continuity and modified-atmosphere packaging must be considered together.

Engineering work

Map preparation, washing, cutting, dewatering and packing. Translate capacity and product requirements into supplier questions and a trial plan, with food-safety validation assigned to the responsible specialists.

Decision deliverables

A process-flow concept, equipment requirement brief and register of trial and validation questions.

03

Drying and dehydration

Best suited forA drying investment or product change that needs a product-specific route and trial plan.

Critical technical points

Feed size, loading, temperature–time history and air conditions shape drying behaviour. Water activity, moisture, texture, colour and energy demand belong in the same product brief.

Engineering work

Compare applicable drying routes; define product-specific trials; record yield, energy and quality assumptions before equipment commitments.

Decision deliverables

A drying-route comparison, trial matrix and supplier enquiry with acceptance targets to be verified.

04

Juice processes

Best suited forA juice line or product brief where yield, pulp, preservation and filling choices must be aligned.

Critical technical points

Fruit condition, extraction yield, pulp specification and enzyme activity influence the process. Oxygen pickup, concentration, preservation and filling route change product-quality and equipment decisions.

Engineering work

Connect preparation, extraction, separation, treatment and filling. Define interfaces and identify the product trials and supplier evidence required for the intended distribution conditions.

Decision deliverables

A process outline, equipment enquiry and register of quality, utility and validation decisions.

05

Product and process development

Best suited forA new formulation or process change moving from a concept into structured trials.

Critical technical points

Formulation, ingredient variability, process window, stability and target unit cost must be defined together. Scale-up changes mixing, heat transfer, residence time and handling.

Engineering work

Set target attributes and a baseline; structure staged trials; separate measured findings from hypotheses and identify what must be checked again before scale-up.

Decision deliverables

A development brief, trial matrix, findings record and next-stage decision brief.

06

Feasibility and implementation support

Best suited forAn investment or implementation decision with competing supplier scopes and unresolved site interfaces.

Critical technical points

Capacity balance, utility demand, labour assumptions, layout and hygienic interfaces must agree with the investment model. Automation boundaries and commissioning acceptance cannot remain implicit.

Engineering work

Compare technical scenarios and supplier scopes; identify missing site data, interfaces and acceptance questions; coordinate agreed project or on-site activities within a written mandate.

Decision deliverables

A technical feasibility note, interface and responsibility register, and staged implementation action list.
